How to Ride

The joust at the tilt

Two riders, one on each side of the tilt, charge from opposite ends. Each crossing is a course; a match is five. The rules are John Tiptoft's ordinances for jousts (England, 1466), adapted to the bike.

Scoring

A hit counts only if the lance breaks on a lawful target. Body (saddle to helm, shield included): 1 spear. Helm: 2. Unhorsing, or breaking coronel to coronel: 3, and unhorsing wins at once. After five courses the judges rank coronel breaks, then hits on the sight of the helm, then spears.

Fouls

Breaking on the saddle: −1. Striking the tilt: −2, then −3, then disqualified. Striking the bike (the horse): −2 with a warning, then disqualified. Striking a rider from behind, or losing the helm twice: disqualified.

Balance and the shield

Hits cost balance; at zero you are unhorsed. Hold the shield to halve the cost of body hits. Raise it on the instant of impact to parry: no break, and the attacker loses half their speed and much of their balance. Two parries can unhorse.

The lists

Build speed, cross, ride on, and brake. You may turn only inside the turning end ahead of you. Brake early for a slow turn; brake late, inside the end, to skid round faster at a cost in balance. Whoever turns first charges first.

Attacks

    A diamond on the rival shows where your tip will land and what it scores. A gate of light on their side shows when to release. The lance is heavy: it follows your aim slowly and strikes once a course. The sword is light: two strokes a course, but you must ride close to the tilt.
